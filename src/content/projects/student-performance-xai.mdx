# SHAP-Based Explainable Student Performance Prediction Using Random Forest, SVM, and Logistic Regression

**Conference Paper – Under Review**  
*Authors: Harsh Khamkar et al.*

---

## Executive Abstract

Predicting secondary student academic performance early in the educational lifecycle is crucial for timely instructional intervention. However, many contemporary machine learning implementations suffer from either opaque "black-box" decision models or subtle historical target leakage (such as incorporating downstream intermediate exam marks).

This study formulates a rigorous, zero-leakage explainable machine learning framework evaluated across combined secondary student cohorts in Mathematics and Portuguese language studies.

## Core Methodologies

- **Multi-Model Benchmark**: Evaluated Random Forest, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Logistic Regression under stratified cross-validation.
- **Explainability**: Integrated TreeSHAP and KernelSHAP to produce global feature importance hierarchies and local waterfall attribution plots.
- **Leakage Prevention**: Strictly filtered downstream test marks to isolate actionable socio-academic, behavioral, and demographic features (e.g., dedicated study time, parental education level, school absences).
- **Educational Guidance**: Provided interpretable decision thresholds tailored for academic advisors to design targeted intervention plans before final evaluations.
